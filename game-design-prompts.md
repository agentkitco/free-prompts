# Game design prompts

Free copy-paste prompts from [AgentKit Co](https://agentkitco.com/free-prompts/). Replace anything in [BRACKETS] with your own details.

## The design brief

**When to use:** Paste this once at the top of a new chat so every answer is scoped to your jam.

```text
DESIGN BRIEF FOR THIS SESSION
- Project: [VIDEO GAME / TABLETOP / MOD for GAME]
- Engine or tools: [ENGINE + VERSION / pen and paper / mod tools]
- Time available: [e.g. 48-hour jam, ends Sunday 6pm my time]
- Team: [solo / names + roles]
- Skills: code [LEVEL], art [LEVEL], audio [LEVEL], writing [LEVEL]
- Jam theme or brief (if any): [THEME + RULES]
- Things I love in games: [2–3 examples of MECHANICS or FEELINGS]

HOW I'D LIKE YOU TO HELP
- Keep everything scoped to my time and skills. Warn me when an idea is too big and suggest a smaller version.
- Keep ideas original. Don't reuse characters, worlds or names from existing games or other media.
- Label all numbers (hours, stats, values) as estimates.
- If you're unsure about an engine feature or a game's modding rules, say so and tell me where to check.
- Short answers with headings, tables or checklists.

Reply "Ready" and wait for my first request.
```

## Theme to ten hooks

**When to use:** The jam theme has just been announced, or you need a fresh idea from a prompt word.

```text
Game-jam theme: [THEME]. Time: [HOURS]. Team: [SOLO / TEAM SIZE + ROLES].
Engine/tool: [ENGINE]. My skills: [SKILLS].
Generate 10 game ideas that interpret the theme in different ways: literal, metaphorical, as a mechanic, as a rule or constraint on the player, and as the setting. For each: a one-sentence pitch, the core verb (what the player does most) and a scope rating (S / M / L).
Then pick the 3 most buildable in my time and say why.
```

## Find the core loop

**When to use:** You have an idea but can't say what the player actually does minute to minute.

```text
Here's my game idea: [IDEA].
Describe its core loop as: player action → feedback → reward or consequence → reason to do it again.
Then suggest 3 alternative core loops for the same idea, and tell me which one is quickest to prototype as a grey-box (no art) in one day.
```

## Scope killer

**When to use:** Your feature list keeps growing and the deadline doesn't move.

```text
Here's my feature list: [FEATURES]. I have [HOURS] in total.
Be ruthless:
1. Estimate rough hours per feature. Label them as guesses.
2. Mark each feature as core-to-the-fun or nice-to-have.
3. Cut until the total fits with 25% of the time left as buffer.
4. Suggest cheap substitutes for expensive features (e.g. text boxes instead of cutscenes, one looping track instead of a soundtrack).
```

## Guide without text

**When to use:** Playtesters get lost or keep missing something important.

```text
Players get lost or miss [THING] in this level: [DESCRIPTION OF LAYOUT AND WHAT GOES WRONG]. Suggest ways to guide them without tutorial text:
lighting, landmarks, colour coding, sightlines, framing, enemy or item placement, sound cues and breadcrumb pickups. Rank the ideas by how cheap they are to add, and say what each one communicates.
```

## Economy sanity check

**When to use:** You have currencies, shops or crafting and want to know if the pacing works.

```text
My in-game economy:
Sources (how players earn [CURRENCY]): [LIST WITH RATES]
Sinks (what they spend it on): [LIST WITH COSTS]
Typical session length: [TIME]
Work out how long it takes to afford each item, showing the maths step by step so I can check it. Flag anything that comes too fast or too slow, and any loop that could generate unlimited currency. Give me the formulas as spreadsheet cells so I can verify.
```

## Dominant-strategy hunt

**When to use:** Before release, to find what min-maxers will exploit.

```text
Here are my game's options with their stats and rules: [PASTE].
Act as a player who only wants to win as efficiently as possible. Find:
- Dominant strategies (always the best choice)
- Options that are never worth picking
- Exploits or degenerate loops For each, suggest the smallest balance change and its likely side effects.
```

## Modding rules checklist

**When to use:** Before you start any mod for any game.

```text
I want to make a mod for [GAME]. Give me a checklist of what to verify before starting:
- The EULA / terms of service and any official modding policy
- Whether official mod tools or an API exist
- Rules on monetisation and donations
- Whether I can redistribute any of the game's assets
- Multiplayer and anti-cheat considerations
- Where mods are allowed to be shared Don't state this game's rules as fact unless you're certain. Tell me where to look instead (official site, EULA, official forums or wiki).
```

## Playtest plan

**When to use:** Before a playtest session, even a casual one with a friend.

```text
I'm running a playtest of [GAME] with [NUMBER] players for about [TIME].
Write a plan:
- Up to 3 questions I'm trying to answer
- What to tell players beforehand (as little as possible)
- What to watch for (hesitation, deaths, backtracking, quitting, smiles)
- What I must not say during play
- A 5-question post-session survey mixing rating scales and open questions
```

## Jam timeline

**When to use:** At the very start of a jam, or a self-imposed deadline.

```text
I have [HOURS] for a game jam that ends at [DEADLINE + TIME ZONE], and I need sleep. Build a timeline with: idea lock, grey-box prototype, core loop playable, content, feature freeze, polish, and build & upload. Leave at least 3 hours before the deadline for building, uploading and testing the uploaded version. Include breaks.
```

## Post-jam retro and next step

**When to use:** After the jam, while it's still fresh.

```text
The jam is over. What happened: [NOTES]. Feedback I received: [PASTE].
Write a retrospective: what worked, what didn't, what I'd do differently, and an honest view on whether this game is worth continuing. If yes, outline a 4-week plan for a post-jam version. If no, give me 3 lessons to take into my next project.
```
